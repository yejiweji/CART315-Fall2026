# Motivation Statement: Walking Simulator

I want to explore how a game can create a feeling of panic and desperation through the environment alone. I'm interested in the uncertainty that comes with having to make a decision without enough information to know whether it's the right one. It's a feeling I experience in everyday situations, like seeing my bus approaching down the street and having to decide whether to run towards my usual stop or try to catch it at the next one. I have to make a choice without knowing which option gives me a better chance, and by the time I find out, it might be too late to change it. I've been thinking about how this feeling could translate into an interactive experience, where the player is free to make decisions but cannot always tell whether they're making progress.

For this project, I want to investigate how lighting, sound, and repetitive architecture can influence a player's sense of direction and control. I'm interested in whether an environment can create tension without relying on explicit storytelling, threats, or complicated mechanics. By placing the player in an endless concrete stairwell with lights that always seem to lead somewhere, I want to explore how the promise of an exit can encourage someone to keep moving even when there's no evidence that they're getting closer.

This also makes me question what it means for a player to have agency when they lack the information needed to make a confident decision. The player can choose to go up or down, but neither direction guarantees progress. I want to experiment with how this uncertainty affects the player's experience, particularly as the stairwell becomes more ominous and harder to navigate. Rather than simply creating a space that looks unsettling, my goal is to investigate whether I can make the player feel trapped through the act of navigating it, and whether the frustration of not knowing what to do can become the central experience of the game.
