# Motivation Statement: Stair Simulator

In my original version (Project #3), I wanted the player to feel lost and unsure of where they were going. I did this by keeping the stairwell empty and repetitive, with very little information to help the player find their way or make sense of where they were. After submitting it, though, I started wondering if I had taken this too far. Maybe adding more familiar details and other people could make the experience feel more real and grounded while also more uncomfortable.

I started thinking about this after experiencing a fire alarm in the condo I recently moved into. I had only been living there for about a week, so I was still unfamiliar with the building. I had been told that tenants would receive an email if a fire alarm was just a test, but when it went off, I hadn't received anything. I didn't know whether it was real or not. Since I live on a higher floor, I also didn't feel like I could wait around to find out. I had to start making my way downstairs in case it was an actual emergency. What made me feel uneasy was not knowing what other people knew or whether they were taking the alarm seriously. I remember hoping I would run into someone who could tell me what was happening, but instead I encountered a panicked mother with a baby. I felt like I had to step up even though I was just as confused. I also remember how long the walk down the stairs felt and how few people I saw along the way.

For Prototype 4, I want to bring some of this experience into the game. I want to add more familiar details to the stairwell, like railings, floor numbers, and fire exit signs, as well as a starting area that gives the player some context. I also want to add a few characters that the player can interact with. I'm interested in what happens when the player tries to get answers from other people but finds out that they don't really know what's going on either. I want to see if these changes can make the experience feel more believable while still keeping the uncertainty from the first version enhancing the panicked experience.
