# Design Journal: Final Jam

## Journal Update #1:
When we were discussing the theme "Stuck in a Loop," I asked my sister what came to mind. Her immediate response was the newborn phase of motherhood. I hadn't initially considered it, but the more we talked about it, the more I could see how fitting it was. Having a newborn means entering a routine that revolves almost entirely around someone else's needs. Feeding, changing, comforting, putting the baby to sleep, and then doing it all over again. There is very little room to think about anything else, let alone yourself. I started thinking about how this kind of repetition could become a metaphor for people pleasing and the experience of constantly putting others before yourself.

The conversation also made me think about my own mother and the way many parents understand love. I've noticed how easily being a good parent can become synonymous with giving everything to your children. The assumption is that the more you sacrifice, the more you love. I understand where this belief comes from, especially when caring for a newborn requires so much attention, but I've started questioning whether completely neglecting yourself is actually beneficial to either parent or child.

Something I've taken away from learning about human development is that taking care of yourself is also part of taking care of your children. A parent who continues to grow as an individual, maintains their own interests, and makes time for their needs has more to give. You cannot continuously pour everything into someone else without eventually running yourself empty. I think there's a misconception that prioritizing yourself means taking something away from the people you love. In reality, looking after your own well-being allows you to be more present for them. Self sacrifice can become counterproductive when it leaves you with nothing to give.

This made me interested in exploring how someone can gradually lose sight of themselves without consciously deciding to. I don't think it necessarily happens through one major sacrifice. It can happen through hundreds of small decisions that seem insignificant on their own. You put something off because someone needs you. You give up an opportunity because someone else comes first. Eventually, you become so accustomed to considering everyone else's needs that you stop asking yourself what you want. I think this is what makes the newborn phase an interesting setting for the game. The mother isn't necessarily unhappy with her child or resentful of her responsibilities. She loves her child, but somewhere along the way, she stops making room for herself.

## Journal Update #2:
I started putting together the apartment using an online asset as the base and some additional furniture for the baby's room. I wanted the apartment to feel cozy and lived in, rather than making it look like the mother isn't taking care of her home. The idea is that she puts a lot of effort into caring for her baby, but very little of that attention goes towards herself. I started thinking about how I could show this through the way the apartment is furnished.

One of the things I added was a long dining table without any chairs. I thought this could suggest that the mother doesn't really make time to sit down and eat for herself. I also made the entrance difficult to get to by placing a lot of things around the front door. I was thinking about how she might have gone a long time without leaving the apartment. I am considering adding piles of garbage near the door to push this idea further, although I don't want the apartment to look neglected in a way that contradicts how much she cares for her baby.

I also started creating a stronger contrast between the baby's room and the mother's bedroom. The nursery is the only room with painted walls, and it has much more furniture and decoration. I added several plants to the nursery as well, since I wanted the signs of care throughout the apartment to be concentrated around the baby. The crib is gold, partly because I wanted it to feel like something especially precious to the mother. I also made the nursery well lit while keeping the rest of the apartment dimmer. The mother's bedroom is the darkest room and has very little furniture or decoration. I wanted the difference between the rooms to reflect how much of her attention goes towards the baby, to the point where she barely considers what would make her own space comfortable.

I'm still thinking about how much of the apartment should revolve around the baby. For example, I could make most of the food, toys, and decorations throughout the house baby related. I think this could make the mother's absence from her own life more noticeable without having to directly tell the player anything. At the same time, I don't want every detail to communicate the exact same thing, so I want to be careful about how I use these objects.

I also started thinking about the player's ability to interact with the environment. Since the game involves clicking on objects to complete everyday tasks, I realized it might not always be obvious what the player is pointing at. I want to add a small point in the centre of the screen so players have a clearer idea of where they are aiming and what they can interact with. I don't want to rely too much on instructions or prompts, since I still want the player to figure things out naturally.
