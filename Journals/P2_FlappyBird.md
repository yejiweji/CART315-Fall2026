# Design Journal: Flappiest Bird

## Journal Update: #1

Initially, I planned to strip away the addictive elements that contributed to Flappy Bird's success to see if the game itself could still hold people's interest. However, I started to feel like removing these elements would only make the game feel unfinished, and the results might be too blatant. If I took away the things that made Flappy Bird engaging and the game became boring, would that really tell me anything I didn't already expect? It felt like I would be proving a point by deliberately making the game worse, rather than genuinely exploring what makes it successful in the first place.

Instead, I started wondering what would happen if I went in the opposite direction. What if I ramped up these elements and deliberately exploited what we know about human behaviour? If the creator of Flappy Bird had not taken it down, but instead used what he learned about player behaviour to keep updating and refining the game, what might it eventually look and feel like? At what point would improving engagement become something more manipulative?

This direction interests me because I want to explore when players become aware of the loop they are caught in. How long would someone keep playing before recognizing how the game encourages them to try again, chase a higher score, or believe that success is just one attempt away? At what point would they realize that their reactions are being deliberately encouraged by the design? Rather than removing the game's appeal, I want to push its most engaging elements further and see whether players would still consider it a good game if they became aware of how it keeps them playing.
