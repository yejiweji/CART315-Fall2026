<br>
One thing I really liked about Lim et al.'s discussion of prototypes as manifestations of design ideas is the idea that we can sometimes learn things about ourselves through the act of creating. We might not even fully understand what we are thinking or feeling until we see it take shape in something we've made. This reminds me a lot of journaling. When journaling, you don't necessarily sit down knowing exactly what you want to say. Sometimes you just start writing and let your thoughts come out as they are. I think prototyping can work in much the same way. It gives us somewhere to put our ideas, even when they are messy, unfinished, or don't entirely make sense yet.
<br>
I think this is also why I appreciate the idea of prototypes as manifestations rather than finished products. They don't have to capture a fully developed idea. They can simply be a starting point, a way of getting something out of our heads and seeing what it becomes. Sometimes, we might discover something through making that we wouldn't have discovered just by thinking about it.
<br>
I also love how much room prototyping gives us to make mistakes. I think we get so caught up in wanting our work to be good or perfect that we forget what it's like to create simply because we enjoy creating. There is something so freeing about realizing that I don't have to make something impressive or even particularly good. I can just make whatever I feel like making, follow an idea, experiment, and see where it takes me. Honestly, it makes me feel almost like a child again. There is an excitement that comes with not knowing what I'll end up with and being able to enjoy the process without worrying so much about the outcome.
<br>
I think this is what makes prototyping feel like a form of self-exploration and discovery. We give ourselves permission to be imperfect and to figure things out as we go. The messy parts of creating can feel intimidating, especially when we are used to wanting everything to be right. But there is also something incredibly liberating about letting go of that expectation. It almost feels like releasing a bird that has spent its entire life in a cage into a vast open sky. There is so much room to go anywhere, and no one predetermined way to get there.
